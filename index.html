    About

    Hi, I'm Clifton. I'm a postdoc in the Gahan Lab at the Centre for Chromosome Biology, University of Galway.

    I want to understand the rules and principles that guide development, and in particular how neurons are made. I come at this from several angles: genetics, single-cell genomics, chromatin and imaging. By comparing very different animals, I hope to learn how that process evolved and how animal complexity arose.

    Right now I work on the starlet sea anemone, Nematostella vectensis. It's a cnidarian, part of the group of animals that includes jellyfish and corals. It has a nerve net rather than a centralised brain, and it sits at a key point in the animal tree of life.

    Current interests

    Chromatin and neural development in Nematostella. I combine chromatin profiling, bulk and single-cell RNA-seq, transgenics and confocal imaging to find out how genome regulation guides cells to become neurons in a cnidarian, the sister group to bilaterian animals such as flies and humans.

    Neural regeneration. Nematostella can regrow its whole body, nervous system included, after being cut in two. A growing part of my work asks how neurons are rebuilt during regeneration, and how that compares with adult neurogenesis, the ongoing production of new neurons in the adult animal.

    Past research

    How monoaminergic neurons develop. For my PhD at the University of Leicester, with Roberto Feuda, I studied how dopamine- and serotonin-making neurons are specified in the Drosophila embryo. Read my thesis ↗

    Comparative biology. As a research associate I built a cell-type atlas of the sponge Sycon ciliatum, and I have worked on evolutionary questions in cicadas, locusts, bush crickets and mosquitoes.
